About the project

Listening to heritage in motion

Montreal is home to a distinctive and diverse Jewish community, including significant populations who identify as Sephardic and Mizrahi, with roots across North Africa and the Middle East. Their cultural heritage is expressed through institutions, religious practices, songs, languages, memories, and orally transmitted musical traditions.

Many of these repertoires are at risk of disappearing. At the same time, contemporary musical expressions, including newly composed songs in Judeo-Arabic, remain comparatively underexplored. This project approaches musical heritage as something living, dynamic, and community-held.

The project begins at the grassroots level, placing community knowledge and agency at the centre of documentation and activation.

Why it matters

More than an archive

The project responds to gaps in scholarship on localized and intangible cultural traditions, while creating a resource for future research and creative engagement. Its community-led ethnographic approach connects scholars and artists with community members and knowledge-holders.

The resulting archive can support historical and biographical research, melodies, transcriptions, oral histories, educational activities, and engagement with diverse musical and linguistic traditions.

By foregrounding transnational histories and lived cultural practices, the project contributes to richer public understandings of minority communities, identity, belonging, migration, and cultural continuity.

Do you carry a song, story, or musical memory?

I am interested in connecting with people who have knowledge of, experience with, or memories connected to Middle Eastern and North African Jewish musical traditions in Montreal. Research context

Community-led and ethically conducted

The project uses community-oriented ethnographic approaches to understand how musical heritage is produced, transmitted, remembered, and activated in the present.
